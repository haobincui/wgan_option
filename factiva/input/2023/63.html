1426 ET - A widely expected Fed decision pushes Treasury yields higher, with the two-year now at 4.228%. The Fed raised its target for the fed funds rate to a range between 4.5% and 4.75%, while a reference to "supply and demand imbalances related to the pandemic" was removed from the statement. "The market had been expecting a more dovish tone in the statement and then a more hawkish tone in the conference," UBS's Mike Cloherty tells WSJ. He says the tweak in the statement is meant to corroborate a reduction in the pace of rate hikes, while Powell will try to keep markets from easing monetary conditions any further. HD U.S. Stocks Whipsaw As Fed Raises Rates By 25 Basis Points, Says Hikes Will Continue -- MarketWatch
WC 274 words
PD 1 February 2023
ET 19:17
SN Dow Jones Institutional News
SC DJDN
LA English
CY Copyright © 2023, Dow Jones & Company, Inc.

LP 

U.S. stocks whipsawed Wednesday as the dollar strengthened and short-term Treasury yields climbed after the Federal Reserve delivered a 25 basis point interest-rate hike and asserted that more would likely follow. The S&P 500 was off 12 points, or 0.3%, at 4,086 shortly after 2 p.m., according to FactSet data. The Nasdaq Composite climbed 7 points, or 0.1%, at 11,597. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 245 points, or 0.7%, to 33,841. The mixed reaction in stocks followed as investors had anticipated that Fed Chairman Jerome Powell would try to dispel expectations for a rate cut later this year. In its statement released following the close of its February meeting, the Fed kept a key line signaling that its plans for tightening monetary policy would continue.
